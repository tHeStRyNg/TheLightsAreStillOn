# The Lights Are Still On

## Storyline Chapter Breakdown

This document defines the narrative structure and chapter flow of **The Lights Are Still On**. It is intended to be used as a reference to ensure story, gameplay, and tone remain consistent throughout development.

---

## Prologue — The Letter

**Setting:** The boy’s home and journey

**Story:**
During school break, a young boy receives a handwritten letter from his grandfather:

> *Come see me.*
> *I don’t have much time.*
> *The lights are still on.*

The letter feels old, as if it had been written long ago but delivered late. Concerned and curious, the boy decides to visit the old town.

During the journey, the world subtly changes. Daylight dulls, the radio cuts out, and the road grows quieter. There is no clear transition—only the feeling that something is off.

**Narrative Purpose:**

* Establish motivation
* Introduce mystery
* Ground the story emotionally

## Chapter 1 — The Town That Never Sleeps

**Setting:** Town entrance and main street

**Story:**
The boy arrives at the town at **1:00 PM**, yet the sky is frozen in a state of perpetual dusk. The sun never rises or sets. Every streetlight and window is illuminated, but the town is completely empty.

When the boy attempts to leave, the road bends back into town. The fog thickens. Escape is impossible.

**Narrative Purpose:**

* Establish the town’s core atmosphere
* Introduce the time anomaly
* Create unease without immediate horror

## Chapter 2 — Grandfather’s House

**Setting:** Old family home

**Story:**
Inside the house, everything is untouched—furniture, photographs, and personal belongings remain as they were.

The boy finds an old lantern resting on a table. Beside it lies a note from his grandfather warning him:

* The light reveals what hides
* Do not look too long
* If it flickers, run

No explanation is given. Only caution.

**Narrative Purpose:**

* Introduce the lantern
* Establish rules without exposition
* Strengthen family connection

## Chapter 3 — The Sky Is Wrong

**Setting:** Streets surrounding the house

**Story:**
With the lantern in hand, the boy explores nearby streets. Clocks show conflicting times. Shadows stretch unnaturally long. The sky remains unchanged.

Using the lantern lightly reveals subtle distortions—walls ripple, shapes vanish when focused on, and reality feels thin.

**Narrative Purpose:**

* Teach lantern mechanics safely
* Allow experimentation
* Build anticipation

## Chapter 4 — Watchers

**Setting:** Alleys and narrow streets

**Story:**
When the spectral light is used for longer periods, figures appear in the darkness—silent watchers with glowing eyes. They do not attack. They simply observe.

The town reacts to prolonged spectral use. Lights flicker. Sounds warp.

**Narrative Purpose:**

* Introduce true horror elements
* Teach restraint and risk
* Reinforce consequence-based mechanics

## Chapter 5 — The First Confrontation

**Setting:** Interior location (shop, hallway, or room)

**Story:**
The boy enters a space that seals behind him. A hostile demon manifests—physical, aggressive, and capable of harming him.

The lantern can reveal and weaken the entity, but it flickers unpredictably. Survival requires composure and careful use of light.

**Narrative Purpose:**

* Establish real danger
* Teach combat and survival rules
* Raise stakes

## Chapter 6 — Bound Souls

**Setting:** Personal and memory-filled locations

**Story:**
The boy encounters bound souls—family members trapped within the ethereal realm. They appear monstrous at first but are revealed to be victims.

Through careful interaction and lantern use, some can be released. Others may remain trapped.

**Narrative Purpose:**

* Shift horror toward tragedy
* Introduce irreversible choices
* Tie mechanics directly to story

## Chapter 7 — Instability

**Setting:** Central town areas

**Story:**
The lantern becomes increasingly unstable. It flickers without warning and reacts emotionally. The town itself grows more hostile.

The boy begins to realize the lantern is not just a tool—it wants to be used.

**Narrative Purpose:**

* Escalate tension
* Undermine player confidence
* Prepare for the climax

## Chapter 8 — The Father

**Setting:** Deep ethereal space

**Story:**
The boy confronts his father, bound by chains of shadow. This encounter is more dangerous and less forgiving.

Release may be impossible. Failure is likely. The lantern behaves erratically.

**Narrative Purpose:**

* Emotional climax
* Confront limits of control
* Reinforce central themes

---

## Chapter 9 — The Town Remembers

**Setting:** Revisited locations

**Story:**
The boy moves through familiar spaces. Depending on earlier choices, some lights go out, some remain on, and the town reflects what has been revealed or ignored.

**Narrative Purpose:**

* Show consequences
* Slow pacing
* Encourage reflection

---

## Final Chapter — Leaving (or Not)

**Setting:** Town edge

**Story:**
The boy reaches the edge of town once more. The outcome depends on player choices:

* The fog may part
* The road may loop
* The lantern may finally go dark

The town remains. The lights may go out—or stay on forever.

**Narrative Purpose:**

* Quiet resolution
* Ambiguity
* Lingering emotional impact

---

## Notes

This structure is designed to:

* Remain finishable for a solo developer
* Support multiple endings without branching complexity
* Keep gameplay and story tightly integrated
